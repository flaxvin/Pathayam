What happened

    The four rules the demo enforces

    The app enforces the same four rules on your real money.

    1. You can only budget what you have

      Ready to Assign starts at what actually arrived. Assign more and it goes negative, and the app keeps saying so until you take some back.

      Build your first budget

    2. Overspending is never hidden

      Push an envelope below zero and it turns red with a cover button. Take it from an envelope with money to spare, or let the rollover handle it under whichever model you picked.

      When you go over

    3. Card spending reserves the cash

      Buy something on the card and the category gives the money up while the payment envelope holds it. The statement is never a surprise, because the money was set aside when you spent it.

      Credit cards

    4. Nothing appears from nowhere

      Every rupee is somewhere: in an envelope, in Ready to Assign, or held for next month. Roll the month and watch leftovers carry over.

      Assign, move and hold

    If you don’t pool your money

    The demo household above shares one budget, which is what most households do. The full demo also has two people keeping their own budgets, with their own accounts, their own envelopes, and ₹40,000 and ₹18,000 a month committed to the shared bills.

    • A private account the rest of the household cannot see at all
    • Rent paid from it, filed to the household’s envelope, with no money transferred
    • A month where he paid for more than he put aside, and the three ways that can be settled
    • Two people leaving in the same month, settled two different ways: one released, and one turned into money the household owes
    • One of them coming back six months later, picking up where they left off

    None of this is switched on for a household that pools everything, so you won’t see a second grid or any extra controls.

    What the full app adds

    The full app includes the features that keep a budget working month after month.

    • Transaction imports. Statement PDFs from twelve banks, CSVs, bank alerts and YNAB or Actual exports, all landing in a review queue.
    • Rules that learn. File a payee twice and a rule is proposed, never applied silently.
    • Loans, investments and net worth, with the firewall that keeps growth out of the budget.
    • Tax, with the AIS already read. The full demo’s tax page carries an invented Annual Information Statement, and a tax-free gains card with something to suggest. It will not take an upload of yours, because everyone visiting shares one household. How to read your own.
    • Undo on everything, for thirty days, from an append-only log.
    • Separate budgets, for households that keep their own accounts and split the shared bills, without moving money between accounts.
    • Asking an AI. Connect Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini to your own instance and ask about your figures, read-only unless you allow it changes. The full demo will not connect one: it issues no tokens and approves no AI app. How to connect.
